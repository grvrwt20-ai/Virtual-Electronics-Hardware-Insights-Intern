Week 3 – Simulation and Prototyping Strategy: Smart Energy Monitoring and Load Management Device

This assignment proposes a Smart Energy Monitoring and Load Management Device for homes, small workshops, and educational laboratories. The concept uses voltage and current sensing, a microcontroller, a local display, and optional Internet of Things connectivity to show electrical measurements and help users understand energy consumption. This is a design and planning document; it does not claim that a physical prototype has been built or that simulations have already been executed.

The report explains the problem, objectives, scope, and hardware architecture. The major functional blocks are isolated voltage sensing, a suitably rated current sensor, signal conditioning, a microcontroller, a regulated low-voltage supply, a display, and optional wireless communication. A functional flow explains how sensor signals would be sampled and processed into estimated power and energy information. The concept is intended for educational demonstration, not as a certified energy meter or protective device.

The simulation strategy proposes using a circuit simulator such as Proteus or LTspice, depending on component-library support. The sensing and signal-conditioning stages would be modelled first, while firmware calculations and alert logic could be tested separately if a complete microcontroller model is unavailable. Planned test parameters include voltage, load current, sensor offset, noise, sampling rate, filtering, and communication availability. Performance indicators include measurement error against a reference instrument, repeatability, update interval, energy-estimation drift, alert latency, power consumption, and successful communication.

The prototyping roadmap covers requirements and component selection, circuit simulation, firmware development, low-voltage integration, calibration, verification, and documentation. Test cases cover baseline readings, several safe load levels, repeatability, noise filtering, network loss, invalid sensor signals, and recovery after power interruption. A proposed five-week timeline defines milestones for each stage. Risks such as measurement error, unsafe electrical connections, component availability, network failure, and data privacy are paired with mitigation measures.

The report concludes with potential improvements such as trend graphs, local data export, anomaly detection, modular hardware, and improved energy-saving recommendations. All performance figures are targets to be verified, not measured results. Any mains-side testing must use properly rated isolated equipment and qualified supervision. The final implementation should be updated with exact component part numbers, simulation screenshots, and recorded test results after the work is performed.
